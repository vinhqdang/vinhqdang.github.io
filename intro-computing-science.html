Introduction to Computing Science

British University Vietnam · BSc (Hons) Data Science and Artificial Intelligence

Interactive lessons that go with the lectures. They are made to be played with: change something, predict what happens, then check.

Week 8 · Systems

The Fetch–Execute Cycle

A Von Neumann computer you can operate: Control Unit, ALU, memory and the PC, MAR, MDR, CIR and ACC registers. Step through any instruction, write your own program, and diagnose programs that go wrong.

Open the simulator → Week 8 · Systems

The Memory Hierarchy

Registers, L1, L2 and L3 cache, RAM and SSD. Watch data move between levels, see cache hits and misses, find out why access order matters, and see the Von Neumann bottleneck as the CPU waits.

Open the simulator → Week 8 · Networks

Networks and the Internet

Build and break a network graph, zoom from a home LAN out to the Internet, follow a message hop by hop with tracert, and see how the Internet grew.

Open the lesson → Week 8 · Networks

Network Protocols

The TCP/IP stack and how headers wrap your data, packet switching versus circuit switching with lost and out-of-order packets, IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, and HTTP.

Computational Intelligence · Slides + code

What is AI? Intelligence, minds and machines

Lecture slides for the Computational Intelligence module: definitions of intelligence and AI, a short history, the Turing test and its latest results, Searle's Chinese Room, ARC-AGI and other tests, two dozen philosophical questions about minds and machines, AI in film and TV from 1927 to 2026, and where AI stands in late 2026. Includes three Python 3.14 demos (an ELIZA-style chatbot, a Chinese Room, a word-level “parrot”).

Limits of Computation · Lecture slides (PDF)

Limits of Computation

What computers cannot do and what they cannot do fast. Turing machines with step-by-step tape runs, the halting problem and its diagonal proof, growth rates, P versus NP, quantum computing and cryptography, then what is happening now: the October and November 2026 claims about the Exponential Time Hypothesis and the Riemann hypothesis (shown with sources and their status), the limits of large language models, and world models (JEPA). About 120 slides with Python 3.14 demos and review questions.
